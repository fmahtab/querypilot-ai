# RetailStar — Business Glossary

This glossary defines key business, retail, and product terms used across RetailStar reporting, merchandising, and analytics. It is intended to help teams — and internal tools — interpret questions consistently when discussing sales, inventory, and category performance.

---

## Retail & Business Metrics

| Term | Definition |
|------|------------|
| **SKU (Stock Keeping Unit)** | A unique identifier assigned to each distinct product RetailStar sells. Used to track sales, inventory, pricing, and returns at the product level. |
| **Unit Price** | The retail selling price of a single SKU at checkout, before discounts or returns. |
| **Cost Price** | The cost RetailStar pays to acquire or produce a SKU. Used to calculate gross margin. |
| **Gross Margin** | The difference between unit price and cost price, expressed as a dollar amount or percentage. A core measure of category and product profitability. |
| **Net Revenue** | Total sales revenue after returns and refunds are deducted. Leadership uses net revenue — not gross sales alone — when evaluating category and store performance. |
| **Units Sold** | The number of individual items sold within a given period, category, store, or channel. |
| **Average Order Value (AOV)** | The average total dollar amount per customer order. Calculated as total net revenue divided by number of orders. |
| **Return Rate** | The percentage of sold units or revenue that is returned, calculated by category, SKU, store, or channel. Elevated return rates often appear in complex kits, specialty tools, or fabric cut-to-order. |
| **Basket Size** | The number of items or total dollar value in a single customer transaction. Workshops and in-store events often increase basket size when customers purchase related supplies. |
| **Stockout** | When a SKU reaches zero available inventory at a store or warehouse and cannot be sold until replenished. Fast-moving staples such as white acrylic paint and 12×12 cardstock are common stockout risks. |
| **Overstock** | Excess inventory of a SKU beyond expected demand, often seen with slow-moving seasonal kits after a peak selling period. |
| **Assortment** | The set of SKUs a store or channel carries. Assortment varies by location based on local demand and category strategy. |
| **Replenishment Item** | A consumable product customers purchase repeatedly, such as acrylic paint, embroidery floss, glue sticks, or yarn. |
| **Seasonal Peak** | A defined period of elevated demand tied to calendar events — e.g., holiday wreath-making, back-to-school classroom supplies, or spring wedding DIY season. |

## Sales Channels & Operations

| Term | Definition |
|------|------------|
| **Physical Store** | A brick-and-mortar RetailStar location where customers browse and purchase in person. Each store maintains its own inventory and sales records. |
| **E-commerce** | Online sales fulfilled via ship-to-home delivery through RetailStar.com. |
| **BOPIS (Buy Online, Pick Up In Store)** | An order placed online and fulfilled at the customer's nearest RetailStar store location. Counted as an online sale but fulfilled from store inventory. |
| **Omnichannel** | RetailStar's combined selling approach across physical stores, e-commerce, and BOPIS. Performance is tracked by channel to compare online vs. in-store trends. |
| **Workshop / Make-and-Take Event** | An in-store class or demo where customers create a project on-site. Drives foot traffic and attach sales of related supplies (e.g., yarn for a knitting demo, stamps for a card-making class). |
| **Seasonal Promotion** | A time-bound marketing campaign tied to calendar events such as back-to-school, holiday crafting, Mother's Day gifting, or summer camp prep. |
| **Region** | A geographic grouping of RetailStar stores used for sales and inventory reporting at a level above individual store locations. |

## Customer Segments

| Term | Definition |
|------|------------|
| **Maker** | Any RetailStar customer who creates with art and craft supplies, regardless of skill level. An umbrella term covering artists, crafters, and DIY enthusiasts. |
| **Fine Artist / Illustrator** | A customer who purchases professional-grade paints, brushes, canvas, and drawing materials for studio or commercial work. |
| **Fiber Artist / Sewist** | A customer engaged in knitting, crochet, quilting, embroidery, or garment sewing. Purchases yarn, fabric, notions, and patterns. |
| **Paper Crafter / Stamper** | A customer who scrapbooks, makes greeting cards, or journals. Purchases cardstock, patterned paper, stamps, ink pads, and embellishments. |
| **Small-Business Maker** | A customer who purchases supplies in volume for resale or production — e.g., Etsy sellers, custom gift shops, or artisan vendors. |
| **Replenishment Shopper** | A repeat customer who returns regularly to restock consumables such as paint, floss, glue, or yarn. |

## Product Categories

| Term | Definition |
|------|------------|
| **Fine Art Supplies** | Professional and hobby-grade materials for painting, drawing, and illustration — including acrylic, oil, watercolor, and gouache paints; canvas; brushes; and drawing media. |
| **Yarn & Needlecraft** | Fiber craft supplies including yarn (worsted, DK, bulky weights), knitting needles, crochet hooks, embroidery floss, Aida cloth, and cross-stitch kits. |
| **Fabric & Sewing** | Textiles and sewing supplies including quilting cotton, flannel, apparel fabric by the yard, fat quarters, thread, interfacing, and notions (zippers, buttons, elastic). |
| **Paper & Scrapbooking** | Cardstock, patterned paper, scrapbook albums, rubber and clear stamps, ink pads, die-cutting machines, dies, embossing folders, stickers, and washi tape. |
| **Jewelry & Beading** | Beads, beading wire, cord, chain, findings (clasps, jump rings, ear wires, crimp beads), and specialized pliers and tools. |
| **Kids & Classroom Crafts** | Age-appropriate supplies for school and home projects — washable markers, construction paper, safety scissors, pom-poms, pipe cleaners, and non-toxic adhesives. |
| **Clay & Modeling** | Polymer clay (e.g., Sculpey, Fimo), air-dry clay, sculpting tools, armatures, and silicone molds. |
| **Home Décor & DIY** | Materials for wreath-making, faux florals, candle-making, wood crafts, and personalized home projects. |
| **Adhesives & Finishes** | Glues and sealants including hot glue, Mod Podge, E6000, spray adhesive, and brush-on or spray varnishes and fixatives. |
| **Craft Tools & Storage** | Reusable tools and organizers — cutting mats, rulers, heat guns, embossing tools, storage bins, and thread caddies. |

## Art & Craft Product Terminology

| Term | Definition |
|------|------------|
| **Medium** | The material used to create art — e.g., acrylic paint, watercolor, oil, graphite, or ink. |
| **Substrate** | The surface an artist works on — e.g., stretched canvas, canvas panel, watercolor block, or bristol board. |
| **Gesso** | A primer applied to canvas or other surfaces before painting to improve paint adhesion and color vibrancy. |
| **Skein** | A loosely coiled length of yarn sold as a single unit. RetailStar tracks yarn sales by skein. |
| **Yarn Weight** | A classification of yarn thickness — e.g., DK (light worsted), worsted, or bulky. Affects gauge, project sizing, and hook/needle selection. |
| **Gauge** | In knitting and crochet, the number of stitches and rows per inch for a given yarn weight and needle/hook size. |
| **Fat Quarter** | A pre-cut fabric piece measuring approximately 18×22 inches, commonly sold in quilting cotton bundles. |
| **Notions** | Small sewing accessories — thread, bobbins, zippers, buttons, elastic, pins, and interfacing. |
| **Findings** | Metal components used in jewelry making — clasps, jump rings, ear wires, crimp beads, and head pins. |
| **Cardstock** | Heavy-weight paper used in card making and scrapbooking. Common sizes at RetailStar include 12×12 and 8.5×11 inches. |
| **Die-Cut / Embossing** | Paper crafting techniques using metal dies or embossed folders with a die-cutting machine to cut or texture paper shapes. |
| **Ink Pad (Dye vs. Pigment)** | Stamp ink types — dye ink dries quickly and is translucent; pigment ink is opaque and slower-drying, often used for embossing. |
| **Embroidery Floss** | Stranded cotton thread sold in skeins, used for cross-stitch, embroidery, and friendship bracelets. |
| **Aida Cloth** | An even-weave fabric with visible holes, used as the base for cross-stitch and counted embroidery. |
| **Project Kit** | A bundled set of materials and instructions for a specific craft project — e.g., a candle-making kit or beginner crochet set. Often seasonal; return rates tend to be higher than for individual supplies. |
| **Classroom Pack** | A bulk quantity of a supply item (e.g., glue sticks, construction paper) packaged for teachers or group use. |
| **Consumable** | A supply item that is used up during a project and must be repurchased — paint, glue, yarn, thread, paper. |
| **Staple Item** | A high-velocity, always-in-stock product that customers expect to find at every RetailStar location — e.g., white acrylic paint, #8 crochet hooks, or school glue. |

## Inventory & Merchandising Terms

| Term | Definition |
|------|------------|
| **Category Manager** | The merchandising lead responsible for buying, pricing, and performance of a specific product category. |
| **Localized Assortment** | Store-specific SKU selection adjusted based on regional demand — e.g., heavier yarn inventory in colder-climate stores. |
| **Cut-to-Order Fabric** | Fabric sold by the yard and cut from a bolt at time of purchase. Returns on cut fabric are restricted or tracked separately due to the customized nature of the sale. |
| **Attach Sale** | A supplementary item purchased alongside a primary product — e.g., buying brushes and gesso when purchasing canvas, or stamps and ink when buying cardstock. |
| **Trend-Driven Item** | A product added to assortment based on current consumer or social media demand — e.g., a viral craft kit or seasonal décor project. |
| **Core Staples** | Products that every RetailStar store is expected to carry regardless of location — foundational supplies across major categories. |
